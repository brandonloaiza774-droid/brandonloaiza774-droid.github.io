Rear diffuser & undertray

I designed this from a blank page: the concept, the SolidWorks surface model and every CFD run. The goal is 40 N of downforce at 12 m/s within the car's tight packaging limits.

The problem

The diffuser has to fit inside the ground clearance set by the suspension team and the space set by the aero team. It also can't dump air into the side tunnels.

Simulation setup · SimScale
Inlet
12 m/s from the front wall
Ground
Moving wall at 12 m/s

Geometry
Diffuser and undertray on their own, without the car body
Solver
Steady-state incompressible, k-ω turbulence model
Mesh
About 7.1 million cells
Convergence
2,000 iterations; forces flat from about iteration 700; velocity residuals ≈ 5×10⁻⁵

Progress

I used pressure distributions and velocity fields to decide each change. My first design made 6 N. After 12 iterations it made 31 N. The current design makes 70 N of downforce for 14 N of drag, well past the 40 N target.

What the flow is doing

These are streamlines colored by velocity. Green and yellow are faster, blue is slower.

Case study: fixing the inlet stall

01 · Observation

The CFD showed the flow staying attached at the inlet, but the boundary layer was slowing way down and stalling at the entrance.

02 · Hypothesis

The inlet angle was too big compared to the tunnel, so the expansion was more than the small tunnel could take in.

03 · Change

I made the expansion smaller and kept everything else the same, so the comparison was fair.

04 · Result

The boundary layer stalled much less, and the next iterations built on that geometry.

Other design moves

  • Strakes. Five strakes at the inlet and four in the diffuser, two of them curved, guide the flow through the expansion.
  • Vortex generators. L-shaped vortex generators at the strake ends target the side-edge vortex, since there's no room for more cross-section.
  • Throat area and angle of attack. I tuned these alongside the expansion angle, one variable at a time.

What's next

Air still leaks out the sides of the diffuser instead of flowing through it, and some exit flow leaves out the bottom. Sealing those edges is the next step.

Validating the CFD

Simulation isn't the final answer. The plan has two stages:

  • Olin wind tunnel. We'll 3D-print the parts and use yarn tufts and smoke to check that the real flow matches the CFD.
  • MIT wind tunnel. Then we'll build a 3/4-scale model of the car and test it in MIT's tunnel.

Personal project · 2006 Lexus IS350

Engine, drivetrain & suspension rebuild

I traced a coolant leak to a failed head gasket and replaced the engine with my dad. We later diagnosed a shifting failure and replaced the transmission. I also replaced the rear differential and put the car on coilovers myself.

Engine

The swap included reconnecting the wiring harness, replacing the radiator and engine mounts, and bleeding the cooling system.

Transmission: stuck in third

On the highway one day, the car wouldn't shift out of third. I revved it almost to redline and it still stayed in third. My dad guessed it was the solenoid box, and the code on the dash confirmed it.

First we tried a junkyard solenoid box. The car still wouldn't shift, so that one was probably bad too. A new solenoid box cost $300–400, and a good used transmission was about $700. The old transmission had 220K miles on it, so we replaced the whole transmission.

Suspension

The worn shocks made the ride rough, so I replaced them with coilovers and set the ride height myself. When the car moved strangely after the repair, I traced it to a badly worn control-arm bushing.
